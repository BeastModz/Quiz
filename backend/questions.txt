1) Theory (50 points):
a) The unitless dimension ‘Reynold’s number’ represents the ratio of two
types of forces, what are they? (2 points)
Inertial forces to viscous forces
b) Give a brief explanation of what the governing laws of fluid flow mean:
i) Conservation of Mass (4 points)
Within a fixed system there cannot be mass created or destroyed so the mass
that enters this system must be the same as the amount stored and the mass
leaving the system
ii) Conservation of Linear / Angular Momentum (4 points)
The conservation of linear and angular momentum says that unless a force
acts on a fluid, it will continue travelling in the same direction with the same
velocity, or rotating the same speed in the case of angular momentum.
c) The left half of the heart pumps blood throughout the body but the right
part is dedicated to one area. What is this area of the body and why is
half of the heart dedicated to it? (5 points)
The lungs receive the output from the right half of the heart. This is so that the
blood can remove carbon dioxide taken from the body and absorb oxygen
from the alveoli which it can then distribute to the body afterward.
3
d) How are the valves within the heart opened and closed? (2 points)
Pressure gradients drive the valves to open and close, the muscle
present do not actually open the valves.
e) What occurs at the respiratory boundary? (3 points)
The capillaries connect to the alveoli meaning that the cardiovascular system
can receive oxygen from the respiratory system and the blood can release
carbon dioxide to the respiratory system.
f) Explain the mechanism of how synovial fluid is stored in a joint and
how it is used to lubricate the joint during motion. (5 points)
Synovial fluid is stored in the cartilage and is squeezed out of the cartilage like
a sponge in order to provide lubrication for a joint in motion. After the motion
the cartilage sucks the synovial fluid back up.
g) Give two advantages of using titanium as an implant material. (4
points)
Good corrosion resistance/oxide layer formation, light weight but strong,
young’s modulus is not as different than that of bone when compared to steel,
non-magnetic, biocompatible, osseointegration
4
h) Briefly explain how volume based computational fluid dynamics can
model flows within the human body and what it means to be ‘mesh
independent’. (5 points)
CFD models fluid flow by making a grid of small elements which each have
their own flow direction and speed, combing many of the elements can give a
bigger picture of what’s happening in flows within the human body. Mesh
independent means that changing the arrangement of the elements will not
affect the results of the simulation noticeably.
i) What is the main function of:
i) Red blood cells (2 points)
Transport oxygen and carbon dioxide
ii) White blood cells (2 points)
Fight infections and eliminate foreign particles
iii) Platelets (2 points)
Stop blood loss / maintain hemostasis
5
j) Draw below how the velocity profile of blood will look as it flows through
a cylindrical blood vessel. Explain any differences between your
velocity profile and that of an inviscid Newtonian fluid shown as an
example. (10 points)
Blood Inviscid Newtonian Fluid
The blood has a more curved shape because blood is a shear-thinning fluid,
meaning as more shear stress is applied, the lower the viscosity of blood will
be. It also has a blunt central area because it has a yield stress, there is not
enough shear stress present in the centre of the flow so that means the blood
will move more as a solid body in the centre of the vessel.
